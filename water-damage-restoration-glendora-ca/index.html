Big Dalton Wash, the dams and your property

Glendora has county flood-control works in its canyons. Big Dalton Dam sits at 2600 Big Dalton Canyon Road, and LA County Flood Control District rehabilitated its sluiceway for flood control, stormwater capture and groundwater recharge. Little Dalton Debris Basin had a seismic retrofit, and Big Dalton Wash got flood-control channel work through the WPA in 1940.

These structures manage canyon runoff, but they do not tell you whether your lot is in a flood zone. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask the city about drainage on your street. If water has entered the house, call, and do not wait for the weather to pass.

Glendora's city water, sewer and permits

Glendora's city page describes a city-run water system with 224 miles of mains and about 13,000 meters. If the leak is at the street or the meter, start with the city Water Division. Anything past the meter is yours.

The sewer system is maintained primarily through the LA County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District. A backup that hits several homes is a report to the city and the district. A single-home backup is more likely your own pipe.

Building and Safety is at 116 E. Foothill Blvd, with permits through the Civic Access portal. Ask what a rebuild needs. California requires a contractor licence at $1,000 or with a permit, and the CSLB site lets you check one before you sign. Water damage repair follows once drying is complete.
