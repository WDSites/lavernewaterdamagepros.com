How to Fix Water Damaged Wood in La Verne, CA

Wood swells when wet and can often shrink back if it dries slowly and evenly. The fix depends on which wood it is, how long it was wet, and whether it has a finish on it.

Call (626) 879-0276

Wood remembers water, so go slowly

To fix water damaged wood in La Verne, CA, remove the moisture gradually and then repair what stayed deformed. Wood absorbs water into its fibers and swells. If it dries evenly, much of that movement reverses. If it dries too fast or stays wet too long, it warps, stains or rots.

The first hours matter most. Stop the source, mop up standing water, lift rugs and furniture off wet wood, and get air moving. Do not aim a heater at a floor or pry boards up before you know what is underneath.

Drying wood well is part of the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, and any company you hire should be able to say whether its crews work to it.

Which wood, which fix

Different wood fails in different ways. This table matches each common situation to the usual repair path.

Wood itemWhat water doesUsual repair path
Solid hardwood floorCupping, then crowning, gapping or bucklingSlow dry, wait for moisture to settle, then sand and refinish, or replace buckled boards
Engineered floorEdges swell, layers separateOften replaced, since the thin top layer does not recover
LaminateCore swells at seamsReplace affected planks
Baseboard and door trimSwelling, finish liftingDry and refinish solid wood, replace pressed fiber
Solid wood furnitureWhite haze, warped panelsDry slowly, rub out haze, re-glue joints
Plywood or OSB subfloorSwelling, delamination, odorDry and test, replace sections that stay soft

A moisture meter reading tells you far more than appearance, and sewage or flood contact usually means replacement.

Floors first: cupped, crowned and buckled

Cupping means board edges are higher than centers. It usually comes from moisture on the underside. Crowning is the reverse and often follows sanding too soon. Buckling is boards lifting off the subfloor, a sign of heavy water.

Dry slowly and wait. Sanding a floor that still holds moisture can leave it flat today and gapped next month. Crews use air movers, dehumidifiers and sometimes floor drying mats, then recheck readings over several days before any sanding is planned.

A repair sequence for a wet wood floor

  1. 1

    Extract and lift

    Remove standing water, rugs and furniture. Lift pad and carpet off wood, and wipe wet surfaces.

  2. 2

    Dry with controlled air

    Run dehumidifiers with gentle air movement. Skip high heat, which can crack boards and trap moisture below.

  3. 3

    Measure until stable

    Check readings against a dry reference board each day until they level off at normal.

  4. 4

    Sand, patch or replace

    Sand and refinish if boards flattened, or swap out buckled sections and blend the finish across the room.

Trim, furniture and the hidden subfloor

Baseboards often show the first sign of a leak. Solid wood trim can be popped off, dried and refinished. Pressed-fiber trim usually cannot be saved. Pulling baseboard also lets you see whether the wall cavity behind it is wet.

For furniture, move it onto blocks, wipe it down and let it dry in moving air out of direct sun. White haze in a finish can often be rubbed out after drying. Warped solid panels may flatten again, but veneered pieces tend to peel.

Subfloor is the part nobody sees. Plywood that stays soft, smells sour or shows dark staining needs replacement, and mold needs only moisture and wood to start. Slab homes have no subfloor on the ground floor, but raised foundations do, and a crew checks it with a meter.

When wood should not be saved

If the water was sewage or came from outside, treat porous wood as contaminated and keep people and pets away. Wood that has been wet for days with a musty smell or visible growth is a mold question, and a physician is the right person for any health concern. Read about mold remediation before disturbing it.

La Verne local note

Old wood in La Verne and the Weber House

The La Verne Heritage Foundation runs Heritage Park, home to the Weber House, which was built in 1885, moved to the park in 1979 and restored by the Foundation starting in 1985. Original wood in a house of that age is the kind that gets repaired and preserved rather than swapped out.

If your own home has older original wood floors, trim or built-ins, tell the crew before work starts. Solid old boards often take slow drying and refinishing better than modern thin-veneer products, and a careful crew will decide what to keep before pulling anything.

City water here runs hard, with city groundwater at 351.7 ppm, so wood near a slow leak may also carry white mineral residue after drying.

Source: La Verne Heritage Foundation website and Historical Marker Database entry for the Weber House, plus City of La Verne 2025 Water Quality Report.
