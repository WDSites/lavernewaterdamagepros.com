How crews choose the route

Dispatch is organized around the address, not the city name on your mailing label. A coordinator asks for the street address, the nearest cross street and any gate or access notes, because freeway closures, gated communities and long driveways all change how fast a crew can reach you.

That is also why we say no arrival time on the phone. We tell you what we know about crew availability and what to do in the meantime, and we update you if that changes.

Three things to find before the next leak

Know where your main water shutoff is. In many homes it sits near the street side of the house, at the water heater or in a utility closet, and in condos it may be a unit valve or a building valve. Finding it in daylight takes a minute. Finding it with water spraying takes much longer.

Know who supplies your water. The table above shows which supplier serves each town, but your bill names the one that serves you. If the problem is a broken service line before your meter, the supplier handles it. If it is after the meter, it is yours, and that is when you call us.

Know your insurer's claim line and your policy type. A renter, a condo owner and a homeowner have different coverage and different people to call first, and having the number saved saves minutes when water is moving.

Why the ZIP codes are listed

ZIP codes are a quick way to tell if your address is in the area we serve, but they are not perfect borders. Some ZIP codes cross city lines, and some addresses carry one town's mailing name while sitting in another town or in unincorporated county land.

If your ZIP is on the list, call and confirm your street address. If it is not on the list but you are close, call anyway. The coordinator will tell you straight away whether a crew can reach you.
