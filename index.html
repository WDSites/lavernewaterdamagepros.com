Emergency water damage restoration for La Verne homes and businesses

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping water, removing it, drying the structure and rebuilding what could not be saved. In La Verne, CA that usually starts with a phone call about a burst supply line, a failed water heater, an overflowing washer, a roof leak after a storm or a ceiling stain that keeps growing. Our emergency line is open 24/7, so the call can happen at midnight or on a holiday.

Speed matters because wet drywall, carpet pad and framing start to break down quickly, and mold needs only moisture and time. The sooner water is shut off and extraction starts, the more of the building can usually be dried in place instead of torn out.

We are a dispatch line, not a showroom. You describe the loss, a coordinator explains the next steps and how scope and price will be set before work begins, and a licensed and insured crew is sent when one is available. Same-day response is the goal, though arrival depends on the crew's schedule and conditions on the road.

If water is still spreading, do this first

These steps are safe for most homeowners and they limit the damage while you wait for the crew. Safety comes before belongings.

  • Stop the sourceShut the fixture valve or the main water valve. If you do not know where the main valve is, find it before the next leak.
  • Think about powerTurn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. Water near outlets or the panel means stay out of the room and call the utility or 911 before anyone else.
  • Lift what you canMove small furniture, electronics, rugs and papers off wet floors. Leave heavy items for the crew.
  • Keep people and pets outWater that came from a toilet, drain or outdoors can carry contamination. Treat it as unsafe to touch.
  • Photograph before cleanupTake wide and close photos of the damage and the source. They help with any insurance claim later.
  • Call the 24/7 lineA coordinator will walk through what is wet and what to do next, any hour of the day.

Water categories and what they change

The category of water decides how much material can be saved. Restoration crews classify water using the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it means for the cleanup
Category 1Supply line, faucet, water heater, rain through a fresh roof leakCleanest water. Extraction and drying usually save drywall, flooring and cabinets if started quickly.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine onlyContaminated. Carpet pad and porous material are often removed, hard surfaces cleaned and sanitized.
Category 3Sewage, river or groundwater flooding, standing water that sat for daysGrossly contaminated. Affected porous material is removed and the area is sanitized. Stay out until trained crews arrive.

Water gets worse with time. Category 1 water left wet for days can become Category 2 or 3.

What La Verne tap water means for leaks and stains

The City of La Verne runs its own water utility. Per the City's 2025 water quality report, roughly a quarter of the supply is pumped from city wells and the rest is bought from Three Valleys Municipal Water District, whose water is treated at the Miramar plant. The same report lists hardness of 351.7 ppm as calcium carbonate for city groundwater and 97 ppm for the Three Valleys water.

That has a practical side. Harder water leaves white mineral crust where it weeps, so a crusty line under a faucet, around a toilet supply or on a water heater fitting is worth a look before it turns into a soaked cabinet. Crust does not prove a leak, but a damp crust does.

If you ever lose pressure or see discolored water at every tap, that is a supply question for the City's utilities office at (909) 596-8744, not a restoration question. If the water is inside your walls, shut your own main valve and call us.

Insurance and your claim, in general terms

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners or renters policy. Slow leaks and neglect often fall outside coverage, and floods typically need their own flood policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer, because wording varies.

The California Department of Insurance advises policyholders to report damage promptly, take reasonable steps to protect the property, keep receipts, and photograph and list damaged items. It also suggests not discarding damaged items or making extensive permanent repairs until the adjuster has had a chance to inspect.

Our crews document the loss with photos, moisture readings and a written scope you can hand to your insurer. We cannot promise a claim will be approved, and we do not speak for your carrier.

Restoration is not waterproofing

Callers sometimes ask whether we can stop water from coming in through a foundation or a failing drain. Restoration dries and repairs what water already damaged. It does not waterproof a foundation, regrade a yard or replace a roof.

If the same wall keeps getting wet, fixing the source is a separate job for the right trade, such as a plumber, roofer or drainage contractor. We will tell you plainly when that is the case, and our crews can dry and repair after the source is fixed.
